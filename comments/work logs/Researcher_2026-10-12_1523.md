## 1. Starting point

The repository began with:

- **The base paper.** Vaidyanathan & Vani Lakshmi (2017), *Sri Lankan J. Appl. Stat.* 17(3), DOI 10.4038/sljastats.v17i3.7902. It estimates finite mixtures of multivariate gamma distributions with independent marginals in three steps: a Wilson–Hilferty transform, MCLUST, and a grid search.
- **The student's first plan (v1).** A document titled "Deep Mixture-of-Experts for Gamma Mixture Parameter Estimation", plus a Python package `dmoegamma`.
  - Method: a DeepSets neural Bayes estimator with a "gate" network and one "expert" head per component.
  - Training: a composite loss (Hungarian-matched MSE + NLL + responsibility cross-entropy + load balancing).
  - Baselines: WH-MCLUST, ECM, direct MLE, method of moments, and MCMC.

## 2. Phase 1: Assessment of the v1 idea (28 Sep)

The README, the v1 methodology, the base paper and the model code were read. The assessment was that, as designed, v1 would not be accepted at a top ML venue (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR) or a top statistics journal. A mid-tier computational-statistics journal was realistic.

Reasons:

1. **Amortization was unmotivated.** The gamma-mixture likelihood is tractable and EM is fast (milliseconds for n ≤ 5000), so a reviewer's first question would be "why not EM?".
2. **The main comparator was weak.** It was the base paper's own method. The strong baselines are well-initialized EM, MLE with restarts, and MCMC.
3. **The "mixture of experts" was hollow in the code.** In `models/experts.py`, every expert read only the global embedding `h`. Responsibilities never reached the experts, the experts did not share weights, and so the output was not equivariant to component order.
4. **"First deep-learning method for gamma mixtures" is novelty by swapping the distribution family.** Set Transformer (2019) and related work had already done amortized mixture-parameter estimation for Gaussians.
5. **The theory was inconsistent.** The composite loss mixed a Hungarian-MSE target with an NLL target, which breaks the Bayes-estimator interpretation.
6. **Scale equivariance was ignored**, and the real-data example (Madawaska) is a single component, i.e. not a mixture at all.
7. **Integrity.** The v1 methodology contained AI-assistant-style text, and its literature list was labelled "verified" without being checked.

## 3. Phase 2: Literature review and redesign (28 Sep – early Oct)

### 3.1 Literature search

Web searches checked the closest work. Details are in `LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`.

**Closest competitors.** All are Gaussian-only, or output cluster assignments only:

| Work | What it does | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Set Transformer / Deep Amortized Clustering (Lee et al. 2019) | Amortized Gaussian mixture fitting | Gaussian only |
| Neural Clustering Processes (Pakman et al., ICML 2020) | Amortized cluster assignment | Assignments, not parameters |
| *Transformers versus the EM Algorithm* (He et al., arXiv 2502.06007) | Theory: attention approximates E and M steps | Gaussian only |
| TGMM (Chen, Wu, Fang, arXiv 2505.11918; code available) | A transformer solves many Gaussian-mixture tasks | Gaussian only. **Most direct competitor** |
| Cluster-PFN (Bhaskaran & Viering, arXiv 2510.24318) | Posterior over K and assignments | Gaussian prior; no parameters |
| Amortized Bayesian Mixture Models (Kucharský & Bürkner, *Stat. Comput.* 2026, arXiv 2501.10229) | Flow posterior + membership classifier | Fixed K; label switching handled by sorting the components |
| Reuter et al. (ICML 2025) | In-context full Bayesian inference | Gaussian mixture is only a side case |

**Lineage the work builds on:**

- *Neural Bayes estimators:* Sainsbury-Dale et al. 2024; the Zammit-Mangion et al. 2025 review.
- *EM-like networks:* Neural EM (Greff et al. 2017); Slot Attention (Locatello et al. 2020); Probabilistic Slot Attention (Kori et al., NeurIPS 2024); deep unfolding.
- *Label switching:* Stephens (2000); Jasra et al. (2005); optimal-transport relabelling (2019); DETR's Hungarian loss.
- *Exponential families and EM:* the exponential-family / Bregman correspondence (Banerjee et al. 2005); EM statistical guarantees (Balakrishnan et al. 2017).

### 3.2 The gap identified

- **G1.** No amortized method handles mixtures whose M-step has no closed form, or whose likelihood is intractable.
- **G2.** No method says which estimator a network learns under label symmetry.
- **G3.** No method is Bayes-like at small n and also efficient at large n.
- **G4.** Amortized methods are rarely applied where amortization is essential: very many small datasets.

### 3.3 Redesign

The project was reframed for an ML venue as **AEM-Net**. The gamma family stays as the flagship application. The new design is in `RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY.md`.

**Contributions:**

- **C1. Architecture.** An EM-structured network. Its E-block computes responsibilities (using the exact log-likelihood when available). Its M-block pools each component's responsibility-weighted sufficient statistics and passes them to one shared expert, which acts as a learned inverse link. The network is invariant to data order, equivariant to component order, and exactly equivariant to scale.
- **C2. Theory.**
  - Thm 1: exact embedding of EM steps.
  - Thm 2: collapse under plain MSE, and ordering bias under sorting.
  - Thm 3: the quotient-loss Bayes estimator equals the fixed point of Stephens' relabelling.
  - Thm 4: hybrid efficiency.
- **C3. Hybrid inference.** Amortized estimate, then O(log n) EM steps.
- **C4. Posterior on the quotient space** (a flow-matching head).
- **C5. Scope.** Many families, unknown number of components, and real data with many small datasets.

**Experiments E1–E9, venue and timeline.** Target NeurIPS 2027, with fallbacks to AISTATS 2028 / TMLR, over a 7-month timeline.

**Correction made during this phase:** the first statement of Thm 2 said that non-equivariant heads "learn an arbitrary labelling" under MSE. This was wrong. With an exchangeable prior, the MSE Bayes estimator collapses for **any** function class. The corrected Thm 2 pairs (a) collapse with (b) the bias that sorting-based relabelling introduces under overlap.

**Files:**

- `LITERATURE_REVIEW.md` is new.
- `RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY.md` was rewritten. The old version was kept, and is now `archive/v1/RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY_v1.md`.

## 4. Phase 3: First implementation of the code base

A new family-agnostic package was built next to v1.

| Area | What was implemented |
|---|---|
| Parameter container | `MixtureParams`: weights plus a dictionary of per-component parameters |
| Families | `Family` interface providing: sampling, torch and numpy densities, unconstrained coordinates, canonicalization, features, sufficient statistics, inverse link, M-step (closed form or numerical), prior. **Families:** gamma, Gaussian (full covariance), Weibull, Student-t, negative binomial, Gaussian-copula gamma, Kibble–Moran bivariate gamma (exact negative-binomial series), g-and-k (likelihood-free), and a right-censoring wrapper |
| Simulator | Draws the number of components, sample size (log-uniform), Dirichlet weights and family-prior parameters; overlap stratification by Monte-Carlo Bayes error; random scale/location augmentation; batching; multi-worker data loader |
| Model | AEM-Net (ISAB encoder, slot initialization with distinct learned seeds, E-block, M-block, shared expert, existence head) and a flow-matching posterior head. Baselines built on the same base class: NBE-DeepSets and Set Transformer |
| Theory check | `ExactEMBlock`: the Thm 1 construction, built from the same E/M code as the model |
| Loss | Quotient (Hungarian) loss with deep supervision, responsibility and existence terms, and the E3 variants (naive / sorted / NLL-only) |
| Training | Trainer: AdamW, cosine schedule, fixed validation suite, checkpoints, standardization statistics for the outputs |
| Inference | Amortized estimator; EM and gradient refinement; real-data adaptation (semi-supervised NLL fine-tuning and the self-consistency loss) |
| Baselines | EM/ECM (k-means++ starts, BIC order selection), direct MLE, adaptive MCMC, NumPyro NUTS (optional), ABC-SMC, neural wrapper, adapters for TGMM and Cluster-PFN (these load the official code and are not re-implemented), sklearn VB |
| Evaluation | Metrics in standardized canonical coordinates, quotient Fréchet mean and Stephens relabelling, SBC / coverage / C2ST, prior-predictive MMD, paired benchmark runner |
| Other | Theory checks (EM contraction estimate, symmetry illustrations); real-data loaders (GHCN-Daily, CAS insurance, Allen spike trains, Sentinel-1, Madawaska); generated configs; CLI scripts; tests |

**Bugs found and fixed in the original v1 code** (they made v1's own tests fail):

- the encoder used `torch.nanmin`, which does not exist in PyTorch;
- a wrong `expand` in the composite loss;
- wrong tensor shapes in one v1 test.

**Verification:** 88 tests passed. A 4-step training run showed the scripts work end to end. These numbers were a wiring check, not results.

## 5. Phase 4: Self-review and fixes

When asked whether the work was satisfactory, the AI assistant reviewed it critically and found seven issues. All seven were then fixed.

| # | Issue found | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overlap-stratified simulation was too slow | Measured 2.8 s vs 0.07 s per batch of 64 (gamma k = 2, up to 6 components) | **Parameter bank** (`simulation/bank.py`): each prior draw gets its Bayes error computed once and is filed into a bin; training resamples parameters and simulates fresh data. Validation uses a held-out part of the bank. Re-measured: same cost as unstratified simulation |
| 2 | The auxiliary losses meant the final network did not minimize the pure quotient loss, so Thm 3 did not strictly apply | Design analysis | **Pure-quotient-loss final phase:** the auxiliary terms fade out over 80–90% of training and are absent in the last 10%; the existence head trains on detached features; checkpoints are selected in that phase only; a validation plateau starts the phase early instead of stopping training. A test checks the gradient equality |
| 3 | E4 ("intractable likelihood") was weak, because copula, Kibble and censored likelihoods are all computable | Design analysis | New **`noisy_gamma`** family (gamma + Gaussian measurement error; no closed-form density). Its numerical density went through two revisions (quantile quadrature failed in the tails, then the α < 1 singularity); the final panelled Gauss–Legendre quadrature was checked against direct integration, with relative error between 1e-14 and 2e-6 |
| 4 | The speed comparison was unfair to EM, which ran serially in numpy | Design analysis | **Batched GPU EM** (`baselines/batched_em.py`), built on the Thm 1 block, plus a batched "network then EM" hybrid. Each batched step is tested to match serial EM |
| 5 | No experiment tested choosing the number of components | Missing config | `e1_order_gamma_k2.yaml`: existence head vs. EM with BIC |
| 6 | EM iteration counts were computed but not saved | Code reading | Benchmark rows now carry `x_em_steps` and `x_em_steps_total`. E2 compares EM run to convergence from the network's estimate against 10 k-means++ restarts |
| 7 | Several code paths had never been executed | Coverage check | Tests for every experiment handler, the flow log-density, self-consistency, adaptation, the GHCN parser, and training for seven more families |

**Bug found by the new tests:** the self-consistency loss crashed, because PyTorch's fused CPU attention has no second-order gradient. It was fixed by forcing the standard ("math") attention kernel inside the flow log-density.

**Verification:** 117 tests passed.

**Topic suggestions given:** six alternative titles (now listed in `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §6). The recommendation was to fix the title only after the E2 and E3 pilots.

## 8. What has NOT been done (please weigh this when reviewing)

1. **No experiments and no trained models.** Every performance statement in the documents is a hypothesis (H1–H8).
2. **Theorems 1–4 are proof sketches.** Theorem 1 is supported by a numerical check of its construction. Theorems 3(iii) and 4 need substantial work. Theorem 4 relies on a local EM-contraction assumption that has not been proven for gamma mixtures (it is checked numerically by `theory/contraction.py`).
3. **Citations marked [verify]** in the literature review have not yet been checked against the publishers.
4. **External baselines** (TGMM, Cluster-PFN, the official BayesFlow ABMM) are adapters only, and depend on obtaining and setting up the authors' code.
5. **Real data have not been downloaded.** The loaders are tested on synthetic files only.
6. **Compute for the full plan** (about 40 models, roughly 150–550 GPU-hours, extrapolated) is not yet confirmed.
7. **Authorship.** The design text and code were AI-drafted. The student must check every part and be able to explain it before any submission.

## 9. Known approximations in the code

- **Kibble–Moran density:** truncated series of 256 terms, sufficient for the default prior; not for correlations near 1.
- **Noisy-gamma density:** numerical quadrature, verified to about 1e-6 relative error. It is used only by the baselines and metrics; the network never sees it.
- **Copula and censored gamma:** torch has no gradient of the incomplete gamma function with respect to the shape, so the shape enters those terms without a gradient. It still gets gradients through the marginal density.
- **ABC-SMC:** the importance weights use a Gaussian approximation to the prior density in unconstrained coordinates.
