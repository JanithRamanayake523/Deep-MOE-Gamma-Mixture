## 1. Motivation and novelty

### R0-C-01 [Fatal] The "no closed-form M-step" motivation is contradicted by the code
- **Where:** `RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY.md` §0, §1 (the family table), §4.5(b); title; log §3.2 gap G1.
- **Problem:** The title is "Mixtures Beyond Closed-Form M-Steps". The gamma M-step is a one-dimensional root of a smooth, monotone function.
- **Evidence:** `simulation/families/gamma.py` solves it exactly. `inverse_digamma_gap` uses Minka's start plus 8 Newton steps, differentiable in torch. `weighted_gamma_mle` converges to a tolerance of 1e-10. The "learned inverse link" (the shared Expert) therefore approximates a scalar function the authors already compute to machine precision.
- **Why it matters:** "No closed form" is not "hard to compute". Any reviewer who opens the code finds this in minutes. The same holds for the Weibull shape, the Student-t ν and the NB dispersion, which are all 1-D solves.
- **Required:** State what the learned expert buys over the exact link. Add the baseline "AEM-Net with the exact inverse link", or drop the title claim.

### R0-C-02 [Fatal] Slot Attention + EM, as the risk register concedes
- **Where:** `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §8, row 1.
- **Problem:** Where the likelihood is intractable, the E-block sets `a = 0`. It is then a query–key attention between ρ_j and the slot state, which is Slot Attention (Locatello 2020). The M-block is a responsibility-weighted pooling followed by a gated MLP update, which is also Slot Attention. Thm 1 supports only the tractable case, where EM is already available.
- **Why it matters:** The claimed novelty sits where the theory does not apply (intractable families), and the theory applies where the novelty is absent (tractable families).
- **Required:** One paragraph naming exactly what is new relative to Slot Attention, TGMM and Neural EM. "Different family" is not enough.

### R0-C-03 [Major] "Intractable" noisy-gamma is not intractable
- **Where:** log §5 item 3; `noisy_gamma.py`; E4; decision D3.
- **Problem:** The authors wrote a quadrature density accurate to 1e-14–2e-6 relative error. A density that can be evaluated to 1e-6 admits direct MLE and likelihood-based EM.
- **Why it matters:** The natural baseline for E4 is direct MLE with the quadrature density already in the repo, not ABC-SMC. ABC-SMC is known to scale poorly, and here it uses a Gaussian-approximated prior in the importance weights (log §9). It is a straw man.
- **Answer to D3:** No, this does not convince me. Use a truly likelihood-free simulator, such as a stochastic-process mixture or g-and-k with a nuisance structure. State honestly that the tractable-likelihood regime is where AEM competes with EM.
- **Required:** Add MLE-with-quadrature as an E4 baseline.

### R0-C-04 [Major] Thm 2(a) and 3(ii) are largely known results restated
- **Where:** methodology §4.3, §4.4.
- **Problem (Thm 2a):** Under an exchangeable prior, the posterior mean under the naive loss is the symmetrized mean, so all components coincide. This has been standard in the label-switching literature since Stephens (2000) and Jasra et al. (2005). Calling it "paper-worthy" overstates it.
- **Problem (Thm 3ii):** As I recall, Stephens' Algorithm 1 is exactly the alternating minimization "align each draw to the current estimate, then re-estimate" for a generic loss. Theorem 3(ii) says the Bayes estimator under the quotient loss is a fixed point of that algorithm for the squared loss. That is close to the algorithm's definition rather than a theorem. I am recalling the paper from memory, so please verify.
- **Required:** Cite the known results and say what is new. The only possibly new statement is 3(iii), the amortized-training consistency, and that is the one the log says "needs real work".

## 2. Theory

### R0-C-05 [Fatal] Thm 1(c) is vacuous, and the "exact embedding" test does not test the network
- **Where:** methodology §4.5; `models/exact.py`; `tests/test_em_embedding.py`.
- **Problem (Thm 1c):** "At infinite capacity the network never has higher Bayes risk than T-step EM." Any universal approximator trained to the Bayes risk satisfies this, because the Bayes estimator minimizes risk over all measurable functions. It says nothing specific to AEM-Net's structure.
- **Problem (the test):** `ExactEMBlock` is a separate hand-written module. It reuses `EBlock` and the static `MBlock.pool`, then applies `family.inverse_link`.
  - It never instantiates `AEMNet`.
  - It never runs the real `MBlock.forward`, which applies `symlog`, a gated MLP update, a LayerNorm residual `slots + res(slots)` and an optional slot self-attention.
  - Setting "U_s = m_s, u = 1" is not a parameter setting of that module. The slot state has width H = 128 while the pooled statistics have a different width, and the residual is unconditional.
  - The test compares two numerical solvers for the same 1-D root (`inverse_link` vs `weighted_gamma_mle`) and finds them equal.
- **Why it matters:** The log says Thm 1 is "supported by a numerical check of its construction". The construction is not the model. The honest statement is "a hand-built EM block exists", not "AEM-Net embeds EM".
- **Required:** Either exhibit weights of the actual `AEMNet` that reproduce an EM step (an explicit weight-setting test), or retreat to a universal-approximation statement and drop "exact".

### R0-C-06 [Fatal] Thm 3(iii) ignores the topological obstruction the authors themselves cite
- **Where:** methodology §4.4(iii) and §4.7.
- **Problem:** Prop 2 cites Dym et al. (ICML 2024) on the impossibility of continuous canonicalization, but only for the flow head. The point estimator has the same problem.
  - The Bayes estimator is a continuous map into the quotient Θ_g/S_g.
  - A continuous network outputs an ordered tuple.
  - For g ≥ 2 and d ≥ 2 (gamma with k ≥ 1 has d = 2k ≥ 2), the unordered configuration space of two distinct points has non-trivial monodromy. There are loops of datasets whose Bayes estimates swap the two components.
  - A continuous network cannot lift such a loop. It must either be discontinuous somewhere or pass through the collapsed locus, which is the very failure of Thm 2(a).
- **Why it matters:** "Assume universality on compact sets" is therefore false for continuous networks. The approximation error cannot be driven to zero, and it concentrates near configurations where components swap. This is a place where hard-to-learn behaviour is guaranteed rather than incidental.
- **Required:** State the obstruction. Analyze or measure where the AEM error concentrates (near ties, near equal weights). Do not claim consistency to the Bayes risk without a caveat.

### R0-C-07 [Major] Thm 3 is stated for fixed g, but the implemented loss is not that loss
- **Where:** `losses/quotient.py`; methodology §4.2, §4.4, §6.1.
- **Problem:** With g ~ Uniform{1..6} unknown to the network, three things differ from the clean quotient metric.
  - The loss is divided by the true g (`per_ds = (matched*cm).sum(1)/cm.sum(1)`), which re-weights datasets by 1/g.
  - It adds a penalty on unmatched slots (`unmatched_pi * pen`).
  - The predicted object lives in S-slot space, not in Θ_g.

  The Bayes estimator under that composite objective is not the Fréchet mean of Θ_g/S_g that Thm 3 characterizes. The coordinate weights (`w_pi`, and the u-standardization from `estimate_u_stats`) are free choices, and the "Bayes estimator" changes with them.
- **Required:** State a theorem about the loss actually optimized, or show numerically that the difference is negligible.

### R0-C-08 [Major] The "pure-L_Q final phase" is not pure
- **Where:** `training/trainer.py`, `losses/quotient.py`, log §5 item 2.
- **Problem:** The log claims the final network "minimizes exactly L_Q". In `QuotientLoss.forward`, only the deep-supervision weight and the responsibility weight are multiplied by `aux_scale`. The existence loss, the optional NLL weight and the flow weight (`cfg.flow`, default 1.0) are not. The flow term is added to `total` when `flow_head` is on.
  - Gradient into the backbone is blocked for the flow only if `flow_detach` stays True.
  - The default NLL weight is 0.
  - The claim therefore holds only for the default configuration. It breaks silently if someone sets `nll>0` or `flow_detach=false`.
  - The trainer also keeps training with weight decay, gradient clipping and a cosine schedule. That is fine in practice, but it is not "minimizes L_Q".
- **Check:** The log says "a test checks the gradient equality". Please state exactly which configuration and which terms that test covers.
- **Required:** Scale or assert-off every non-L_Q term in the final phase, or say "approximately".

### R0-C-09 [Major] Thm 4 is a restatement of "EM converges to the MLE", with an error in the stated rate
- **Where:** methodology §4.6; `inference/refine.py`.
- **Problem (content):** The conclusion holds for any initializer inside the contraction basin. The network only supplies the initializer. The efficiency comes entirely from EM, so the "hybrid efficiency" is not a property of AEM-Net.
- **Problem (rate):** The claim is K_n ≥ log n / (2 log(1/κ)). That gives κ^K ≤ n^(-1/2), which is O(n^(-1/2)), not the o(n^(-1/2)) that Slutsky needs. The schedule needs an extra slack factor.
- **Problem (code vs theorem):** `default_em_steps` uses ⌈log₂ n⌉ and calls it "the Thm 4 schedule". That satisfies κ^K ≤ n^(-1/2) only if κ ≤ 2^(-1/2) ≈ 0.71. The theorem's own formula gives, for n = 10⁴:

| κ | steps the theorem needs | error left after ⌈log₂ n⌉ = 14 steps |
|---|---|---|
| 0.71 | about 13 | 0.008 (n^(-1/2) = 0.01) |
| 0.80 | about 21 | 0.044 |
| 0.90 | about 44 | 0.23 |
| 0.95 | about 90 | 0.49 |
| 0.99 | about 460 | 0.87 |

  EM on overlapping mixtures routinely has κ above 0.9, and the experiments deliberately include a high-overlap stratum. The default schedule fails exactly there.
- **Problem (assumption B):** Amortized consistency "as n → ∞" is assumed for a network trained only for n ∈ [20, 10⁴] with finite capacity. That is not plausible, and you do not test it.
- **Required:** Use the κ-dependent schedule, or run EM to convergence. Admit that Thm 4 is the one-step-estimator folklore (Le Cam) with EM as the one-step.

### R0-C-10 [Major] The contraction check underestimates κ
- **Where:** `theory/contraction.py`.
- **Problem:** κ̂ is the median and max of ‖M(θ)−θ̂‖/‖θ−θ̂‖ over 20 random isotropic directions.
  - For a linearized EM map with Jacobian J, this ratio is an average of eigenvalues weighted by the random direction's components. It is not the spectral radius ρ(J).
  - A random direction has projection of order 1/√dim on the slowest eigendirection, so the ratio is biased low.
  - The quantity that governs the number of EM steps is ρ(J), and the check may be in a non-normal regime where the Euclidean ratio is not even monotone.
  - The radii up to 0.8 (in prior-standardized units) are not "local".
  - A single local MLE is used as the centre, and "median" is not a relevant summary for a worst-case rate.
- **Required:** Estimate ρ(J) by power iteration on the EM Jacobian, or by finite-difference eigenanalysis, at the MLE. Report it by overlap stratum.

## 3. Experimental design (nothing run, so these are design flaws)

### R0-C-11 [Fatal] H7 and the "many small datasets" story fail on the authors' own numbers
- **Where:** log §2 item 1 and §6; methodology H7/E7; `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §7.
- **Problem:** The log itself says EM takes "milliseconds for n ≤ 5000". H7 claims at least 100× lower total compute than EM *including training*, on at least 10⁴ datasets. The numbers do not support that.
  - 10⁴ datasets × about 10 ms × 10 restarts is under 20 CPU-minutes.
  - Training is 3–14 GPU-hours per model (extrapolated). The break-even sits near 10⁶–10⁷ datasets.
  - The E7 datasets are 10⁴ (GHCN, Allen) and 10²–10³ (insurance). That is two to four orders of magnitude below break-even.
  - The batched GPU EM baseline (item 4 of the log) makes it worse. Once EM is batched on a GPU, 10⁴ small datasets take seconds.
- **Why it matters:** H7 will be falsified by arithmetic, before any experiment.
- **Required:** Compute the break-even number of datasets now, with a measured GPU EM throughput. Choose a workload above it (for example per-pixel SAR with 10⁶ patches), or give up the amortization-cost argument.

### R0-C-12 [Major] H2 is circular: beating MLE under the training prior is not evidence
- **Where:** methodology RQ2/H2, E1, E2.
- **Problem:** The network is trained to minimize expected quotient loss under the simulation prior, and tested on data drawn from that prior, scored with the same loss. At small n, the MLE for mixtures is poorly behaved (unbounded likelihood, degenerate components). Any prior-informed estimator beats it on that metric.
- **Why it matters:** It is not a surprise, and it says nothing about real data.
- **Required:** The honest target is the exact Bayes estimator under the same prior (relabelled NUTS posterior-mean), which AEM-Net can approach but not beat. Also add a regularized baseline (MAP-EM with a matching prior). Report "gap to Bayes", not "wins against MLE".

### R0-C-13 [Major] The E3 reference ("match NUTS") is ill-posed under overlap stratification
- **Where:** `simulation/bank.py`; E3, H3.
- **Problem:** The training prior is stratified by a Monte-Carlo estimate of the Bayes error. That is a non-product prior with no tractable density, and the bank is a finite empirical sample. NUTS needs the exact prior density. Without it, "matches the relabelled NUTS mean within Monte-Carlo error" cannot be defined.
- **Also:** Each (g, bin) cell holds 20,000 draws (`per_cell`), 5% held out. 200k–10⁶ training datasets over about 15 cells means each parameter vector is reused many times. The network then learns the Bayes estimator for the empirical bank prior, not the continuous one.
- **Required:** Run E3 under a simple analytic prior with no stratification. Report the bank-reuse factor and a test on freshly drawn parameters.

### R0-C-14 [Major] Statistical protocol will overstate significance
- **Where:** methodology §7 (common protocol), E1.
- **Problem (unit of analysis):** Paired Wilcoxon tests over 500 replications per cell treat random datasets as the sample. But the model has only 3 training seeds. Training-seed variance is the dominant source of uncertainty and is ignored, so p-values are anti-conservative. With thousands of paired datasets, everything will be significant.
- **Problem (thresholds):** The falsification thresholds are arbitrary and some are below noise.
  - H4 "< 2% change" is probably below seed-to-seed variation with n = 3 seeds.
  - H6 requires coverage within ±2%. With 500 replications, the standard error of a 90% coverage estimate is √(0.9×0.1/500) ≈ 1.3%, so a perfectly calibrated model fails the ±2% test about 13% of the time per parameter, and almost surely across the many parameters tested.
  - H1 ("≥ 2 of 3 non-Gaussian families") does not say which three.
- **Required:** Use effect sizes with intervals that include training-seed variance (at least 5 seeds, or a hierarchical model). Pre-register margins.

### R0-C-15 [Major] The CRB in E2 is ill-defined
- **Where:** E2.
- **Problem:** The Cramér–Rao bound applies to unbiased estimators and is a pointwise bound. The plotted quantity is a Bayes risk averaged over a prior, for biased estimators, at small n.
  - The Fisher information of a mixture is nearly singular for overlapping or low-weight components, so the average of tr I⁻¹ over the prior is dominated by the hardest draws.
  - The prior is truncated at `min_weight = 0.02`, which is a design choice that changes the answer. It also means a real rare component (heavy-tail insurance severities, say) lies outside the training prior.
  - The coordinates (log-parameters vs original) change the trace.
- **Required:** Drop the CRB curve, or define it conditionally on ϑ, in stated coordinates, as a reference only.

### R0-C-16 [Major] The real-data plan ignores how those data break the model
- **Where:** E7.
- **Problem:** Daily GHCN precipitation is rounded to a fixed resolution (many ties and tiny values), is autocorrelated, has a seasonal cycle, and is thresholded at "wet days". Inter-spike intervals are serially dependent (bursting). The simulator generates continuous i.i.d. draws, so the covariate shift is structural, not something a prior-predictive MMD can fix.
- **Also:** Held-out log-likelihood versus EM cannot show superiority, since EM maximizes training likelihood. The best case is parity, and the cost argument for parity fails (R0-C-11).
- **Required:** Decide the realistic claim for E7 and test the discretization effect in simulation first.

### R0-C-17 [Major] Scope and compute figures are inconsistent
- **Where:** `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §7; methodology §6.2–6.3; `utils/config.py`; log §6.
- **Problem:**
  - Compute is extrapolated from CPU timings to GPU with an assumed 20–100× speedup. That is unmeasured.
  - The measured cost is for batch 64, but methodology §6.3 uses batch 128 and "1–2 GPU-days per family", which contradicts the overview's 3–14 GPU-hours per model.
  - The code default is 100,000 steps; the overview assumes 200,000.
  - The "about 40 models" count is families × baselines × ablations. It ignores that the main results use "one network per k" and "a separate network per family" (§6.2), which multiplies the number of main models (families × k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5}).
  - It also ignores "3 seeds for each final model" and the "≤ 40 trials" hyperparameter search (§6.3). Multiplied out, the plan is several times the 150–550 GPU-hour figure.
  - Test counts go 88 → 117 → 98 passed + 3 skipped. The log does not explain the drop (presumably the archived v1 tests), but a reader cannot tell.
- **Required:** Measure on a GPU before planning. Recompute the total with seeds, k and the search. Explain the test count.

## 4. Implementation and verification

### R0-C-18 [Major] The tests are wiring checks, not statistical validation
- **Where:** log §4, §5, §6; `tests/`.
- **Problem:** About 820 lines of tests cover 5,400 lines of source. "98 passed" and "4-step training run" show that the pipeline runs. They say nothing about whether the estimator is correct, calibrated or better than a baseline. The log is honest that these are wiring checks, but the verification language ("verified", "tested", "implemented; symmetry/equivariance unit-tested") invites a stronger reading than the evidence supports.
- **Missing:** a recovery test on a case with a known answer, such as EM from the truth; a coverage test of the flow head on an analytic case; a check that the network's output approaches the exact posterior mean for a conjugate toy mixture.

### R0-C-19 [Major] Known gradient bias in exactly the "intractable" families
- **Where:** log §9; `gamma.py` (`log_sf_marginals`), `copula_gamma.py`, `censored.py`.
- **Problem:** The shape enters the survival term with `a.detach()`, so no gradient reaches the shape from censored observations. The log says the shape "still gets gradients through the marginal density". But for a censored point, the survival term is the only place the data constrains the shape, so for heavily censored data the E-block's likelihood signal for the shape is simply missing, which biases the learned E-step. This affects the censored and copula families, which are central to E4.
- **Required:** Quantify with a finite-difference check, or implement the gradient (the derivative of the regularized incomplete gamma function with respect to the shape has a known series).

### R0-C-20 [Minor] Truncation and approximation limits left unquantified
- Kibble–Moran: 256-term series, not valid near correlation → 1. State the training prior's correlation range and show the series error across it.
- Noisy-gamma: stated error is 1e-14–2e-6 (log §5 item 3) but "about 1e-6" (log §9). Say which, and where in the parameter space the larger error occurs. Relative error in the far tail affects responsibilities.
- ABC-SMC uses a Gaussian approximation to the prior in its importance weights (log §9). That biases the main intractable-case baseline and weakens E4.

### R0-C-21 [Minor] The flow head is trained against a moving target
- **Where:** `losses/quotient.py` (flow section); methodology §4.7.
- Targets are "aligned to the network's own point estimate", which changes during training. A flow trained on a drifting pivot can be calibrated only if the pivot has converged. Prop 2's claim of valid credible sets on the quotient assumes a fixed pivot. Train the flow in a second stage on a frozen network, or show that results do not depend on it.

## 6. Answers to the open decisions D1–D5

- **D1 (lead with method or theory).** Lead with neither yet. The method's novelty is thin (R0-C-02) and the theory is mostly known (R0-C-04) or obstructed (R0-C-06). The defensible core is a **negative-plus-positive empirical and theoretical study of what neural estimators learn under label symmetry**. That needs E3 done properly (R0-C-13) before anything else.
- **D2 (narrow Thm 3(iii) and 4 now).** Yes. Prove g = 2, k = 1 first (ordered 1-D case, where there is no monodromy). State Thm 4 as a corollary of the one-step estimator, with the corrected rate (R0-C-09).
- **D3 (is noisy gamma convincing).** No (R0-C-03).
- **D4 (NeurIPS 2027 or AISTATS / TMLR).** The NeurIPS target is not realistic. With seven months, nothing run, and the issues above, aim for TMLR or an SBI workshop first. Revisit after E2 and E3 are real.
- **D5 (what to cut).** Cut to gamma (k = 1, 2) and Gaussian only, plus one truly likelihood-free family. Cut the 18-way ablation to 4–5 variants. Defer the flow head and the Prop 2 claims until the point estimator is solid. Drop the CRB curve (R0-C-15).
